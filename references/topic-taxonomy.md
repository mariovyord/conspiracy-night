# Topic Taxonomy

## Content Categories

The Conspiracy Night website organizes conspiracy theories into seven thematic categories. This taxonomy helps users browse by interest and ensures content is properly labeled.

## Categories

### 1. Urban Planning (`urban-planning`)

**Description:** Theories about city design, infrastructure, and movement control.

**Core Themes:**
- 15-minute cities
- Smart cities
- Walkable neighborhoods
- Car-free zones
- Public transportation control
- Neighborhood surveillance
- Urban development conspiracies

**Example Topics:**
- "15-Minute Cities: Convenience or Control?"
- "Smart Cities: Connected or Controlled?"
- "The War on Cars"
- "Public Transit Tracking"

**Typical Concerns:**
- Movement restriction
- Privacy invasion
- Property rights
- Freedom of travel
- Government overreach

**Tone Notes:**
- Often politically charged
- Requires balanced urban planning perspective
- Consider real concerns about city design

---

### 2. Health (`health`)

**Description:** Medical conspiracies, public health theories, and wellness claims.

**Core Themes:**
- COVID-19 origins and responses
- Vaccine skepticism
- Pharmaceutical industry
- Alternative medicine
- Health surveillance
- Medical establishment control
- Disease creation/suppression

**Example Topics:**
- "COVID-19: Lab Leak or Natural?"
- "Vaccine Side Effects Cover-Ups"
- "Big Pharma's Profit Motive"
- "Suppressed Natural Cures"
- "5G and Health Effects"

**Typical Concerns:**
- Medical autonomy
- Corporate profit over health
- Government mandates
- Side effect transparency
- Alternative treatment suppression

**⚠️ IMPORTANT:**
- **Always include health disclaimer**
- **Danger level typically 4-5**
- Emphasize medical professional consultation
- Present scientific consensus clearly
- Avoid anything that could delay medical care

**Tone Notes:**
- Most sensitive category
- Require strong disclaimers
- Balance empathy for concerns with safety
- Never provide medical advice

## Category Usage Guidelines

### Assigning Categories

**Single Category Rule:**
Each topic should have ONE primary category, even if it overlaps multiple themes.

**Decision Process:**
1. What is the core claim about?
2. Which concern is most prominent?
3. Where would users expect to find this?

**Examples:**
- "5G Towers and COVID" → **health** (primary concern is health)
- "Smart City Surveillance" → **urban-planning** (primary concern is city design)
- "Digital Vaccine Passports" → **health** (health is primary, surveillance is secondary)

### Cross-Category Topics

Some topics span multiple categories. Handle this by:
1. Choose the primary category
2. Mention related themes in content
3. Link to related topics in other categories

**Example:**
"CBDCs and Health Data" 
- Primary: **money** (financial system)
- Secondary: **health** (medical privacy)
- Content: Discuss both angles
- Related links: Link to health surveillance topics

## Special Considerations

### Category Balance

Aim for roughly balanced content across categories:
- Don't over-emphasize one category
- Each category should have 3-10 topics
- Fill gaps in underrepresented categories

### Category Popularity

Expected popularity (for prioritizing featured topics):
1. **Culture** (most entertaining)
2. **Paranoia** (most relatable)
3. **Surveillance** (most relevant)
4. **Media** (current events)
5. **Money** (practical concerns)
6. **Health** (controversial but important)
7. **Urban Planning** (niche but interesting)

### Category Safety

Danger level by category (average):
- **Health**: 4-5 (highest risk)
- **Money**: 3-4 (financial decisions)
- **Urban Planning**: 2-3 (political but low harm)
- **Media**: 2-3 (polarizing but safe)
- **Surveillance**: 2-3 (realistic concerns)
- **Culture**: 1-3 (mostly harmless)
- **Paranoia**: 1-2 (philosophical fun)
